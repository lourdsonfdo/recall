Respiratory Therapy Quick Reference

Adult unless noted. Gas pressures in mmHg; airway pressures in cm H₂O. Use fractions in O₂ equations.

01 / ABGs & acid–base

pH7.35–7.45PaCO₂35–45
HCO₃⁻22–26PaO₂*80–100
BE−2 to +2EtCO₂35–45

HCO₃⁻ and BE in mEq/L. *Room air; varies with age and altitude.

Interpret: identify what explains the pH; compare measured with expected compensation. Normal pH does not rule out a mixed disorder.

Acute resp. acidosis: from PaCO₂ ≈40, a 10 mmHg rise lowers pH by ≈0.08 and raises HCO₃⁻ by ≈1 mEq/L.

COPD/hypercapnia risk: initial SpO₂ 88–92% pending blood gases.

02 / Oxygenation

Alveolar gas (simplified): PAO₂ ≈ FiO₂ × (PB − 47) − PaCO₂/0.8; use 47 mmHg at 37 °C.

A–a = PAO₂ − PaO₂; expected on room air ≈ (age in yr + 10)/4 mmHg.

Intubated ARDS, PEEP ≥5: P/F = PaO₂/FiO₂. Mild >200 to 300; moderate >100 to 200; severe ≤100. P/F alone does not diagnose ARDS.

CaO₂ = 1.34 × Hb × SaO₂ + 0.003 × PaO₂. Hb in g/dL; result typically 16–22 mL O₂/dL.

Delivered FiO₂ varies with breathing, fit and device. Keep reservoir inflated on inspiration.

Venturi air:O₂: 28% ≈10:1; 35% ≈5:1; 40% ≈3:1; 60% ≈1:1. Total flow ≈ O₂ inlet flow × (air parts + 1); use adapter instructions.

Cylinder duration (min) ≈ (gauge − reserve in psig) × factor ÷ L/min. D 0.16; E 0.28; H/K ≈3.14 L/psig.

CO poisoning: SpO₂ can look normal. Confirm COHb by blood co-oximetry; give 100% source O₂ until symptom-free.

04 / Pulmonary function

ERS/ATS: FEV₁/FVC < LLN = obstruction; TLC < LLN = restriction. LLN = 5th percentile. Positive response: ΔFEV₁ or ΔFVC >10% of its predicted value.

Disease criteria: GOLD COPD uses post-BD FEV₁/FVC <0.70. Adult GINA asthma: FEV₁ or FVC rises ≥12% and ≥200 mL from baseline.

05 / Mechanical ventilation

PBW (kg) = base + 2.3 × (height in inches − 60); base = 50 men, 45.5 women.

Adult ARDS: VT target 6 mL/kg PBW (adjust 4–8); Pplat ≤30. Driving pressure = Pplat − total PEEP; assess, not a universal cutoff.

Compliance: Cstat = VT/(Pplat − total PEEP); Cdyn = VT/(PIP − PEEP). Use mL for mL/cm H₂O.

Resistance: Raw = (PIP − Pplat)/flow in L/s (passive, constant inspiratory flow). At equal flow, wider gap = ↑ resistance. At equal VT and total PEEP, ↑ Pplat = ↓ compliance.

Minute / alveolar ventilation: V̇E = VT × f; V̇A = (VT − VD) × f. Volumes in L, rate in breaths/min; result in L/min.

Estimated new rate: fnew ≈ fold × PaCO₂measured/PaCO₂desired at constant CO₂ production, VT and dead space.

Auto-PEEP: passive end-expiratory hold measures total PEEP; subtract set PEEP. Allow more exhalation time; treat bronchospasm.

Bilevel NIV: pressure support = IPAP − EPAP. ↑ IPAP at fixed EPAP can improve ventilation; EPAP may improve oxygenation.

06 / Liberation from ventilation

SBT screen: cause improving, adequate oxygenation on modest support, stable hemodynamics, able to initiate breaths.

RSBI = f/VT (L); <105 breaths/min/L is historical, not required before an SBT.

SBT: usually 30–120 min, with or without low pressure support (≤8); T-piece is an option. Do not increase FiO₂ for the trial.

07 / Artificial airway

Cuff by manometer: adult 20–30; pediatric usually <20–25 cm H₂O.

Adult ETT: typical starting depth ~21 cm women, ~23 cm men at incisors; confirm placement and depth. CXR tip ≈4 ± 2 cm above carina.

Peds ETT ID (mm): age in years. Uncuffed age/4 + 4 (>1 yr); cuffed age/4 + 3.5 (>2 yr). Use age/length tables for smaller children.

Suction: lowest effective vacuum; magnitude <200 mmHg adult, <120 mmHg pediatric/neonatal. Limit application to 15 s.

Head injury + GCS ≤8: urgent airway assessment. Unstable tension PTX: unilateral ↓ breath sounds, sudden hypotension; decompress per protocol without waiting for imaging.

09 / Medications & aerosol

Severe asthma: silent chest or normal/rising PaCO₂ is ominous.

Albuterol: usual adult neb dose 2.5 mg. ICS: rinse mouth after use. LABA: never alone in asthma. Acetylcysteine: can cause bronchospasm.

pMDI: slow, deep breath; hold 5–10 s as able; use compatible spacer. DPI: quick, forceful inhalation per device. Particles 1–5 µm can deposit in conducting airways.

10 / Neonatal & pediatric

Apgar: 5 signs × 0–2 points, at 1 and 5 min. 5-min score (term/late preterm): 7–10 reassuring, 4–6 moderately abnormal, 0–3 low. If <7, repeat q5 min to 20 min. Not an initial resuscitation trigger.

Neonatal resuscitation: apnea/gasping or HR <100/min after initial steps → PPV 30–60 inflations/min. If HR remains <60/min after 30 s of ventilation that moves the chest, start 3:1 compressions to inflations. No routine meconium suction.

Typical newborn: HR 120–160/min; RR 30–60/min.

Croup: bark/stridor → dexamethasone; add nebulized epinephrine if moderate–severe. Epiglottitis: fever, drooling, dysphagia → urgent expert airway help; no bedside throat exam.

11 / Infection prevention

VAP: pneumonia >48 h after intubation. HOB 30–45° when feasible; minimize sedation, assess daily for SBT; brush teeth without routine chlorhexidine.

Airborne: TB, measles; negative-pressure room + fitted N95 or higher. Varicella: Airborne + Contact.

Droplet: influenza, pertussis; mask on entry. Contact: C. diff, inpatient MRSA, pediatric RSV; gown + gloves. Add these to Standard Precautions.

Spaulding: reusable equipment contacting mucous membranes or nonintact skin is semicritical: at least high-level disinfection.

12 / Blood gas & oxygenation, continued

Anion gap (no K⁺): Na⁺ − (Cl⁻ + HCO₃⁻); most-cited 10–14 mEq/L. Fixed acid consumes HCO₃⁻ → ↑ gap (lactic/ketoacidosis). · Adult sea-level room-air PaO₂: mild 60–79 mmHg; moderate 40–59 mmHg; severe <40 mmHg. Consider barometric pressure. · Low V/Q: usually improves on O₂; little PaO₂ response to high FiO₂ suggests shunt. PEEP/CPAP may recruit perfused collapsed alveoli → ↑ oxygenation. · Mixed venous: SvO₂ ≈75%, usually 68–77%; PvO₂ ≈40 mmHg. ↓ SvO₂ suggests ↑ extraction (↓ delivery or ↑ consumption). · O₂ delivery = blood O₂ content × cardiac output. ↓ CO may ↓ delivery despite stable oxygenation. · Hb: most-cited pooled 12–16 g/dL; sex-specific ranges also taught. Anemia may ↓ O₂ capacity despite apparently normal oxygenation. · Pulse ox: significant methemoglobinemia pulls SpO₂ toward 85% (can read low above 85%, high below 85%). Motion/poor perfusion/nail polish distort; dark pigmentation can read falsely high. · Waveform capnography confirms tracheal placement. Sudden loss: disconnect, total obstruction or misplaced tube. Hypoventilation ↑ EtCO₂. CPR: EtCO₂ <10 mmHg → reassess compressions; abrupt rise suggests ROSC. · Radial ABG: check ulnar collateral flow before puncture; expel air promptly (↓ PaCO₂, PaO₂ toward ≈150 mmHg either way). Routine room-temp plastic syringe: analyze within 30 min.

13 / O₂ & gas therapy, continued

Heated HFNC: independent FiO₂ 0.21–1.0; L/min: adult 1–40+ (systems up to 60 L/min), child 1–20, infant 1–8. Closed mouth: pharyngeal pressure ↑ ≈1 cm H₂O per 10 L/min. · Heliox (O₂-calibrated meter): actual flow = indicated flow × factor: He/O₂ 80/20 → 1.8; 70/30 → 1.6; 60/40 → 1.4.

Neonatal PaO₂ target: 50–70 mmHg. After birth, preductal SpO₂ (right hand or wrist):

14 / Ventilator modes & management

Assist-control: time/patient triggers → full mandatory breaths; total rate can exceed set. · SIMV: set synchronized mandatory rate + spontaneous breaths; PS on spontaneous breaths ↓ tube/circuit work. · Pressure trigger: nearer zero → less effort; insensitive → miss efforts; oversensitive → trigger without effort. NIV: check for leak too. · Timing: cycle time (s) = 60/rate; Ti = cycle time × I/(I + E). I:E → % inspiratory time: 1:2 → 33%, 1:3 → 25%, 1:4 → 20%. Constant flow (L/min) = 60 × VT (L)/flow-delivery time (s). · ↓ Compliance: volume control: same set VT, ↑ PIP; pressure control: same set inspiratory pressure, ↓ exhaled VT (also ↓ with ↑ resistance). · Alarms: high: secretions, kinked/bitten ETT, bronchospasm, cough, ↓ compliance; low + low exhaled VT: leak/disconnect. Recall limits 10–15 cm H₂O above/below observed PIP. · Dead space: anatomic ≈1 mL/lb PBW. VD/VT = (PaCO₂ − mixed-expired PCO₂)/PaCO₂; usual 0.25–0.35; >0.60 → inefficient ventilation, may hinder weaning. · HFOV: oxygenation: mean airway pressure/FiO₂. ↑ PaCO₂ → ↑ amplitude or ↓ frequency (↑ volume/oscillation). Check chest wiggle. · PEEP recruits alveoli → ↑ FRC/oxygenation. Excess may ↓ venous return/CO/BP, ↑ barotrauma risk.

15 / Weaning & extubation, continued

Weaning indices: NIF −20 to −30 cm H₂O or more negative (more negative = stronger inspiration); VC >10 mL/kg; minute ventilation usually <10 L/min. · Stop SBT: worsening distress, sweats, accessory muscle use, unstable vitals. One Recall protocol: RR >35/min for 5 min; SpO₂ <90%; HR >140/min or ≥120% baseline; SBP >180 or <90 mmHg. · Extubation: high stridor risk → cuff-leak test. fail → systemic steroid at least 4 h pre-extubation. Post-extubation stridor: cool humidified O₂ + neb racemic epinephrine.

16 / Airway, bronchoscopy & procedures

Mucus plug: lobar collapse, trachea toward affected side, ↓ breath sounds. Retained secretions: try cough/chest PT/PEP before bronchoscopy. · Bronchoscopy: Dx: airway inspection, biopsy, BAL. Tx: clear plug causing lobar collapse, remove foreign body, control bleeding. · Foreign body: suspect aspiration with sudden cough in previously well child, esp. unilateral wheeze. Bronchoscopy may find/remove it.

Incentive spirometry: needs cooperation, IC ≈ one-third predicted; if unable, Recall suggests IPPB/intermittent CPAP.

17 / Hemodynamics & ECMO

ECMO: VV (venous return): gas exchange; native heart circulates. VA (arterial return): gas exchange + perfusion. · MAP ≈ (SBP + 2 × DBP)/3; Recall ≥60 mmHg for tissue perfusion, ≥65 during targeted temperature management. CPP = MAP − ICP; most-cited TBI goal ≥60 mmHg. · PCWP >18 mmHg supports cardiogenic edema; normal/low with bilateral infiltrates + hypoxemia supports noncardiogenic edema (e.g., ARDS). 18 mmHg: old AECC cutoff. · SVR = 80 × (MAP − CVP)/CO; usual 900–1,400 dyn·s/cm⁵. PVR = 80 × (mean PAP − PCWP)/CO; usual 110–250 dyn·s/cm⁵. · Pulmonary HTN (Recall): mean PAP >25 mmHg at rest; >30 mmHg on exertion. · Adult spontaneous RR: 12–20/min. CO = HR × SV; CI = CO/body surface area.

18 / Medications, continued

Before paralysis: sedate + analgesia. Succinylcholine: depolarizing; hyperkalemia risk. Reversal: neostigmine (nondepolarizing block), sugammadex (rocuronium/vecuronium), naloxone (opioids), flumazenil (benzodiazepines). · Inhaled NO: selective pulm. vasodilator. Recall term neonatal PPHN: start 20 ppm; monitor inspired NO₂/blood metHb. Gradual wean avoids rebound pulm. HTN. · Neb racemic epinephrine: mucosal vasoconstriction ↓ upper-airway swelling; onset ≈3–5 min, duration under 3 h. · Salmeterol: onset ≈20 min, lasts ≈12 h. Formoterol: onset ≈3 min, lasts up to 12 h. · Ipratropium (COPD maintenance): MDI 17 mcg/puff, 2 puffs four times daily, or SVN 0.5 mg four times daily. Dry mouth common. Tiotropium HandiHaler: 18 mcg once daily. · Albuterol: tremor/tachycardia/hypokalemia possible. Reassess substantial HR rise (Recall: usually 20% from baseline; also 20 beats/min). · Dornase alfa (Pulmozyme): breaks down retained CF secretion DNA → ↓ sputum viscosity. · Theophylline: therapeutic serum 5–15 mcg/mL; early toxicity may cause nausea/abdominal pain; severe toxicity may cause seizures.

19 / Neonatal, pediatric & resuscitation

Surfactant: starts ≈24 weeks, matures ≈35 weeks (L/S ≈2:1: mature). Neonatal RDS: low volumes, ground glass, air bronchograms; Recall Tx: CPAP, intratracheal surfactant. · PPHN: foot oxygenation below right hand suggests R→L ductal shunt. OI = mean airway pressure × FiO₂ fraction × 100/PaO₂. Recall most-cited neonatal ECMO consideration: OI 20–40. · Preterm SpO₂: Recall usual 90–95%. Silverman–Andersen: upper-chest motion, lower-chest and xiphoid retractions, nasal flaring, expiratory grunting: 0–2 each; total 0–10. ≥7 signals impending respiratory failure. · Infant RSV bronchiolitis: apnea possible; nasal suction; O₂ for hypoxemia. Recall prevention: palivizumab for high-risk infants (incl. chronic lung disease of prematurity). · CPR: 100–120 compressions/min; adult 5–6 cm deep. No advanced airway: adult 30:2; child/infant 30:2 one rescuer, 15:2 two. Advanced airway: continuous compressions; adult 1 breath every 6 s (some Recall decks: 6–8 s). · VF/pulseless VT: shock → resume CPR; Recall: arrest epinephrine, amiodarone for refractory VF/pulseless VT. PEA/asystole: CPR + epinephrine; no defibrillation.

20 / PFTs & sleep

Peak flow (% personal best): green 80–100%; yellow 50–80%; red <50%. Most-cited Recall table includes exactly 80% in both green and yellow. · DLCO: obstruction + low → suspect emphysema; normal → consider asthma/chronic bronchitis. Restriction + low → suspect ILD; normal → consider chest-wall/neuromuscular disease. · Lung volumes: TLC = VC + RV; FRC = ERV + RV; IC = VT + IRV; VC = IRV + VT + ERV. · Adult AHI (events/h): mild 5–15; moderate 15–30; severe >30. Recall OSA: ≥5/h with symptoms or ≥15/h without; events predominantly obstructive.

21 / Infection, burns, assessment & QC

Pneumonic consolidation: dull percussion, bronchial breath sounds, ↑ tactile fremitus, clearer whispered-word transmission. · Burn area: adult rule of nines; child age-adjusted chart. Smoke exposure + hoarseness, stridor or oral soot → urgent airway assessment. · Hand hygiene: most effective HAI prevention. After C. difficile care: soap/water (alcohol rub doesn’t kill spores). · Blood-gas analyzer QC: calibration check: acidotic/normal/alkalotic controls. POC calibration fail → retry new cartridge. Second fail → pull from service, use another analyzer/lab.
